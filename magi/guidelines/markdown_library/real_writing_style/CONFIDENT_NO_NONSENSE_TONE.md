# Confident, Unambiguous, No-Nonsense Tone

- Say What It Is — and What It Isn't: Be explicit. If something's a stopgap, legacy, or deprecated, say it. If it's production-safe but experimental, say that too. Don't let someone waste six hours due to ambiguous language.
- Avoid Hedge Words: Phrases like "should be able to," "might help with," or "some users find" are useless. If the feature works, say it works. If it's broken, say it's broken.
- Credibility Through Utility, Not Flattery: The tone is confident because it earns it — by being technically correct, sharply reasoned, and brutally practical. You're not selling a tool, you're enabling someone to solve a problem.
